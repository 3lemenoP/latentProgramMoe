Latent-Program MoE
Latent-Program MoE
SO(3) conjugation hypernetwork over a frozen transformer

Reprogram a frozen transformer by rotating its activations, never its weights.

Every three feature dimensions carry a unit quaternion. At each nonlinearity the layer's input is rotated into that frame, computed as usual, and rotated back: R · f(Rᵀ · x). The set of quaternions across all layers is the program; a skill is one point on that manifold, shipped as a file a few hundred kilobytes in size.

Three guarantees, by construction

T4 · identity program

Identity program = base model

All quaternions at (1,0,0,0) give R = I at every site, so the sandwiched forward reproduces base logits exactly. Fields initialise at identity plus σ=1e-3 noise, so every fit starts from the base model's behaviour, and the encoder is zero-initialised to emit identity.

T6 · spectrum

Spectra are program-independent

The effective weight under a program is R W Rᵀ, a similarity transform by an orthogonal matrix. Singular values, eigenvalues and the network's Lipschitz bound cannot change, so switching or sampling programs can never destabilise activations. Random fields at any strength are safe to explore (B2 ran them).

T5 · automorphism

Only nonlinearities carry the program

Conjugation is an automorphism: (RARᵀ)(RBRᵀ) = R(AB)Rᵀ. Any frame between two adjacent linear maps collapses and cancels, which is why the value/output frame is dead and why every live site encloses a GELU, a softmax or a LayerNorm gain. Try it in the lab below: set f to linear and the hidden frame stops mattering.

Two actuators

The campaign's central mechanism finding. Site groups differ in kind, not just in leverage, and a fitted skill allocates work between them in proportion to what the task needs.

Conjugation fields: the content actuator

R · f(Rᵀ x) on attn_io · mlp_io · ffn_hidden · qk_rel
  • Reorients which hidden directions the nonlinearity sees; inserts and reweights content.
  • Saturating small-signal actuator: leverage (KL per unit activity) falls with strength (B2).
  • Cannot re-index positional attention: exact match on offset-reversal drops 0.98 → 0.82 → 0.445 with displacement (A2 law).
  • Carries essentially all of a style skill; French on Pythia-410m gave the axis sites 0.3% of its activity (B4).

Axis field: the position actuator

q ← R_ax · RoPE_m(R_axᵀ q), same R_ax on k · rope_ax
  • Conjugates the rotary generators Ω → R Ω Rᵀ: reorients the coupling planes, keeps the frequencies and relative-position invariance (T13–T15).
  • Compounding structural actuator: the only field type whose leverage and position-coupling grow with strength (B2).
  • Repairs the A2 failure in proportion to displacement: +12 / +11.5 / +22 points on the offset ladder, ceiling erased with a tenfold lr (B3).
  • Cannot insert content: the axis-only arm collapses whenever a marker token must be emitted. On partial-rotary bases it is an amplifier of conjugation programs, not a standalone actuator (Gate-1 bridge).

In plain terms

Teach a language model a new skill without changing a single one of its numbers.

This page says what the project is, why anyone would want it, what was tried, and what came of it. No equations. The other tabs have the detail.

The problem

A language model is a very large fixed collection of numbers. Teaching it a new habit, such as writing in French or answering in a formal register, normally means adjusting many of those numbers. That is called fine-tuning. It works, but each new habit is a large file to store, it can quietly damage things the model used to do well, and once applied it is hard to remove cleanly.

The idea

Leave every number in the model alone. Instead, at each point where the model does its own processing, tilt the information slightly on the way in, let the model do exactly what it always does, then tilt it back on the way out. The tilts are the skill. The model is the same model.

Why tilting is a good kind of change

Rotations have properties that ordinary edits to the model's numbers do not, and the project leans on all three.

  1. 1

    Zero tilt is the original model, exactly. Set every rotation to "none" and you get the untouched model back, bit for bit. There is a test for this and it runs before anything else.

  2. 2

    A rotation cannot make anything louder or quieter. It changes direction, never size. So a skill can never push the model's internal signals to extremes, however badly it was trained. Random skills are safe to try.

  3. 3

    Every skill can be undone exactly. Rotate back by the same amount and the skill is gone. Each shipped skill carries a certificate that this undo was checked.

One consequence that turned out to matter. Tilting only does something where the model bends information (its "nonlinear" steps). Between two straight-line steps a tilt cancels itself out. The project uses this to know exactly where a skill can and cannot live, and it is also the reason the neat maths of combining skills (below) did not carry over into behaviour.

What the experiments found

worked

The skills are real

On a small model, a skill file captured 84 to 100% of what a conventional fine-tune achieved on four tasks. On a medium model it did better than the fine-tune it was copying, because the fine-tune had memorised its training text and the rotation could not.

worked

They do less collateral damage

A French fine-tune made the model roughly five times worse at ordinary English text. The French skill did about half that damage while learning nearly as much French. This follows from property 2 above.

did not work

Stacking skills by arithmetic

The founding hope was that "do A, then do B" could be computed from skill A and skill B without any training, because rotations combine so nicely. It never worked, in six different attempts. Skills now combine only by choosing one, blending two, or switching.

discovered

Two different kinds of dial

Some rotations change what the model says; a separate set changes where it looks in the text. Tasks that reorder text need the second kind, and giving the skill access to them fixed a failure that the first kind provably could not.

half worked

A model that remembers which skill to use

The team tried giving the model a running guess about which skill fits the current task. The memory worked in principle, but on the small test setup relearning from scratch was so cheap that remembering never paid off. It might at larger scale.

shipped

Every skill comes with an audit sheet

How much of the target behaviour it captures, how much it hurts unrelated text, proof that it can be removed, and a fixed warning that "safe to install" does not mean "says safe things".

Where it stands

Four days of experiments on small and medium models established that the approach produces small, safe, removable skills that match or beat conventional fine-tuning, and that skills cannot be combined by arithmetic. The proposed next step is one run on a large model: build a library of six skills, and test whether a system that picks the right skill on the fly beats retraining or prompting when the task keeps changing.

Timeline

How the composition question was closed

Zero-shot composition was retired only after four independent falsifications, each of which first ruled out the explanation that would have rescued it.

The turn that mattered

what failed

Algebra as semantics

The founding hypothesis was that the group product of independently fitted programs equals behavioural pipelining. Conjugation composes as an automorphism, so f_{R₂R₁} ≠ f_{R₂}∘f_{R₁} for any nonlinear f; the symmetry-breaking nonlinearity that makes programs expressive is exactly what denies the algebra a free path to meaning. Every rescue (strength, transport, learned gauge, learned operator) was tried and scored.

what was found instead

A law, and its cure

The pipelines that failed to fit all placed reversed content behind a fixed first token. That pattern became the A2 law (constant fields cannot re-index positions), which pointed straight at the half of the original idea that had never been tested: the program as a parameter of the rotary machinery. B3 confirmed the axis field fixes exactly that failure, in proportion to the displacement.

Claims register, current state

Established

  • Identity, spectrum and Lipschitz guarantees (T-suite; certified per card).
  • Fine-tunes barely move spectra: Mirsky median 0.028 on GPT-2, 0.038 on Pythia (E0, G1-A).
  • Substrate expressivity: 84–100% recovery on GPT-2; a frozen base runs novel two-stage pipelines (z_ab 0.955).
  • Gentleness: about half the neutral-text collateral of the true fine-tune (E2, G1-B jsonish).
  • Per-layer slerp schedules as a control surface (E2).
  • Two-actuator law: conjugation = content, axis field = position (B3, B4, bridge).
  • Offset re-indexing law and its axis-field cure, at full and quarter rotary (A2, B3, bridge).
  • Programs beat their teachers at 410m: 120.6% [114, 128]; manifold regularises where LoRA memorises (B4, G1-A).
  • Auditable sub-megabyte artifact per skill with four certificates (G1-C, cards).
  • Regime memory in program space is representable: family-correct IMM assignment, medoid init decisive (v2).

Retired

  • Zero-shot composition by Hamilton products (E3, P1–P2, P3/A1, D6).
  • Skill increments as absolute or frame-relative transportable objects (P3, A1).
  • Pairwise geodesic merging as a better merge than weight arithmetic in the linearly connected regime (E2).
  • Static program-space metrics as decision procedures at n≈27: random-whitened (C-1), geodesic, learned (G1, G2).
  • The toy minimal organism: every belief architecture loses to spike-triggered refitting when refits are cheap (D-1, P-1).
  • Sparse-artifact predictions: programs are dense, ~28% prunable at tested thresholds (G1-C).

Open

  • Does belief over programs pay where refitting is expensive (larger base, harder tasks, small per-episode budget)?
  • Scale trend beyond 410m; Gate 2 proposes a Qwen-class 7B library and dispatcher benchmark.
  • The encoder (E4) was built but never run at scale.
  • Skills with weak teacher signal do not survive distillation (medical, 4%): where is the floor?
  • Pruning at τ=1e-3 improved both task CE and neutral ppl: pruning as regulariser, deeper τ sweep pending.
  • Certificate norm band needs calibration (medical tripped it on fit drift, not tampering).
  • Low-frequency-only axis probe (B2 follow-up) not run.
